Days 61-90: "Then I measure. 5-8 KPIs tied to what the business cares about. I build a one-page dashboard and present it. That dashboard is the governance program's insurance policy."

2. Recovery Plan (continued)

What to Stop, Simplify, and Remove

Stop
  • Governance forums that produce status, not decisions
  • Governing all data assets equally
  • Processes that add cycle time without reducing measurable risk
Simplify
  • Data Owner role: from RACI to 5-bullet charter
  • Catalogue tool: 50 critical assets, not everything
  • Approval gates: light touch early, rigorous where risk is highest
Remove
  • Meeting formats that lack decision authority or clear outcomes
  • Complex artifacts that no one reads
  • Any process the business routinely bypasses

"When business teams bypass governance, that is a signal that the process is wrong, not that the people are wrong."

3. Adoption & Operating Reset

From Gate to Service

From (Current)To (Reset)
Governance as a gate: approve or reject→Governance as a service: enable and improve
Councils as status meetings→Forums with decision authority
All data governed equally→Tiered governance: priority data first
Data Owners as title holders→Data Owners as decision-makers
Governance polices compliance→Governance partners with delivery
No measurement of value→Quarterly reporting tied to business outcomes
Embedded in Delivery

At my current organization, I embedded governance into tier-one delivery projects so data quality gates were part of the build, not a review after the fact. Product teams saw governance as a quality accelerator, not a speed bump.

Structured Re-engagement

Monthly governance communications, quarterly stakeholder satisfaction surveys, and lunch-and-learn sessions to rebuild awareness and drive adoption from the ground up.

Aligned with Leadership

This plan is a starting framework, not a mandate. In week one, I align priorities with the Director and calibrate against what the organization needs most urgently. The plan adapts to the environment.

Change the Question

Executives ask: "Is governance worth it?"
The right question: "What does it cost us when governance fails?"

Stop Saying
"Data governance ensures compliance"
"We need more Data Stewards"
"The framework requires adoption"

Start Saying
"Governance reduced incidents by X% this quarter"
"We need 3 people to fix the $Y supply chain problem"
"Here is what governance delivered: [specific wins]"
